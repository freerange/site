Week 923
========

Another busy couple of weeks working on [Mavis][]. School flu vaccination season is well under way, so we're seeing increased usage of the system - and consequently more queries and performance issues to address. Our [work](/week-921) to allow SAIS teams to run clinics as well as school sessions was [covered in the i newspaper](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/i-weekend/20260919/281706916586400) too.

We took a break from the project to meet some of our fellow CoTech members at the [autumn gathering][]. The theme of the event was "AI, Communities & Coops". There were some guest speakers, non-co-op folks and a bunch of representatives from various member co-ops. It was good to catch up with some old friends and hear how people are facing up to their changing work. For co-ops that are used to charging for their time the promise (whether realised or not) of a technology that allows you to do more work in the same amount of time creates a lot of difficulties, both internally and in conversations with clients.

On the whole I was a little surprised at how enthusiastically some co-ops have adopted the new "AI" technology. But knowing how thoughtful and honest our CoTech friends are I could see how much everyone was struggling with the new reality. I think the Communication Workers Union's recent report [AI Workers' Inquiry 2026](https://techworkersinquiry.org/ai/) reflects more closely what I am experiencing - this technology moves work around rather than reduces it, and has many negative externalities.

I've been enjoying watching some of Zig developer [Andrew Kelley's](https://andrewkelley.me/) conference talks. He seems like a thoughtful and empathetic community leader unlike some of those we have to tolerate in the Ruby/Rails community. His talk [Making Systems Programming Accessible](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qncdi-Fg0-I) put into words one of the many reasons I enjoy working with [Chris](/chris-roos) and [James](/james-mead) - they are unafraid to delve deep into systems to try to understand the root cause of problems. For example James recently solved an issue with flaky tests by understanding what [DatabaseCleaner](https://github.com/DatabaseCleaner/database_cleaner) was doing, how it interacted with ActiveRecord and why we'd introduced it in the first place. It was also a good reminder that I'm happy doing this too, and the more time I can carve out for [deep work](https://www.ethicalbooksearch.com/uk/books/m/ol:OL17713267W-is:9780315222144-is:9781455586660/deep-work-cal-newport) the more peaceful I am.

Have an excellent weekend, folks.

-- Chris
